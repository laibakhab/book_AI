# Introduction to Physical AI

Physical AI represents a paradigm shift from traditional disembodied artificial intelligence toward embodied intelligence that interacts with the physical world. Unlike conventional AI systems that process abstract data, Physical AI systems must perceive, reason, and act in real-world environments.

## What is Physical AI?

Physical AI combines machine learning, robotics, and engineering to create systems capable of intelligent behavior in physical spaces. It encompasses:

- **Embodiment**: Intelligence emerges from the interaction between the system and its physical environment
- **Real-world Perception**: Systems must interpret sensory data from cameras, lidars, tactile sensors, and other physical sensors
- **Action and Manipulation**: Intelligent systems must be able to perform meaningful tasks in physical space
- **Learning from Interaction**: Systems improve through continuous engagement with their environment

## Key Components of Physical AI

### Perception Systems
Physical AI systems rely on sophisticated sensor fusion to understand their environment. This includes:

- Visual recognition systems
- Spatial mapping capabilities
- Multimodal sensing (touch, sound, temperature)
- Real-time processing algorithms

### Reasoning and Planning
Physical AI systems must make decisions considering:
- Environmental constraints and dynamics
- Physical laws (gravity, friction, momentum)
- Task objectives and safety requirements
- Resource limitations

### Action Execution
Executing plans in the real world requires:
- Motor control and coordination
- Fine-grained manipulation skills
- Adaptive behavior to handle environmental changes
- Error recovery mechanisms

## Applications of Physical AI

Physical AI is transforming numerous industries:

- Manufacturing and automation
- Healthcare robotics
- Autonomous vehicles
- Domestic service robots
- Space exploration
- Agriculture and environmental monitoring

:::note
Physical AI differs fundamentally from traditional AI in that intelligence emerges from the interaction between the system and its physical embodiment. This creates unique challenges but also tremendous opportunities.
:::
